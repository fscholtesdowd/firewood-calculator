Red Maple vs Shagbark Hickory Firewood: Which Burns Better?

Shagbark Hickory carries about 58% more heat per cord than Red Maple (28.6 vs 18.1 million BTU) - here is the rest of what actually differs when you burn one over the other.

The verdict

Red Maple is ready to burn in 9 months; Shagbark Hickory needs 12 - 3 more months stacked before it is dry enough to burn clean. Red Maple splits easy; Shagbark Hickory splits medium. Smoke and spark behavior are the same class on both - low smoke, low sparks. Shagbark Hickory holds a longer, hotter coal bed overnight (excellent vs Red Maple's fair).

It is a real trade: Shagbark Hickory wins on heat, Red Maple wins on how soon you can burn it. Pick Shagbark Hickory if you are stocking for next winter, Red Maple if you need wood dry sooner.

About Red Maple

Soft maple. Noticeably less heat than sugar maple -- worth knowing which one you actually have.

About Shagbark Hickory

The benchmark for a long overnight burn. Splits cleanly when green, far harder once dry.

Heat values are millions of BTU per full cord (128 cubic feet stacked) of air-dried wood at roughly 20% moisture content. Figures are taken from Utah State University Extension's Wood Heating firewood table, cross-checked where both cover a species against University of Illinois Extension's Characteristics of Firewood. Cord and rick dimensions, seasoned weight per cord, and the splitting, smoke and spark ratings draw on University of Missouri Extension G5450, Wood Fuel for Heating. Published tables disagree with each other by a million BTU or more on some species, and real output depends far more on how dry your wood actually is - treat these as a ranking and a planning estimate, not a laboratory measurement.
