Quaking Aspen vs Tamarack (Larch) Firewood: Which Burns Better?

Tamarack (Larch) carries about 48% more heat per cord than Quaking Aspen (21.8 vs 14.7 million BTU) - here is the rest of what actually differs when you burn one over the other.

The verdict

Quaking Aspen is ready to burn in 6 months; Tamarack (Larch) needs 9 - 3 more months stacked before it is dry enough to burn clean. Neither is a chore to split - both rate easy. Quaking Aspen throws low sparks against Tamarack (Larch)'s medium. Tamarack (Larch) holds a longer, hotter coal bed overnight (fair vs Quaking Aspen's poor).

It is a real trade: Tamarack (Larch) wins on heat, Quaking Aspen wins on how soon you can burn it. Pick Tamarack (Larch) if you are stocking for next winter, Quaking Aspen if you need wood dry sooner.

About Quaking Aspen

Very light wood. You will burn roughly twice the volume of oak for the same heat.

About Tamarack (Larch)

The hottest common softwood -- outperforms several hardwoods. Pops more than a hardwood.

Heat values are millions of BTU per full cord (128 cubic feet stacked) of air-dried wood at roughly 20% moisture content. Figures are taken from Utah State University Extension's Wood Heating firewood table, cross-checked where both cover a species against University of Illinois Extension's Characteristics of Firewood. Cord and rick dimensions, seasoned weight per cord, and the splitting, smoke and spark ratings draw on University of Missouri Extension G5450, Wood Fuel for Heating. Published tables disagree with each other by a million BTU or more on some species, and real output depends far more on how dry your wood actually is - treat these as a ranking and a planning estimate, not a laboratory measurement.
