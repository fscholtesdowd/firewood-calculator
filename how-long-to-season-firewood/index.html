How long does firewood take to season?

Between 6 and 24 months, and which end you land on depends almost entirely on the species.

What actually matters

Split it first. Bark is built to keep water in. A round dries slowly no matter how long it sits; the same wood split dries several times faster because you have opened the end grain and the faces.

Off the ground, top covered, sides open. Wood needs airflow more than shelter. A tarp wrapped all the way around a stack traps moisture and grows mould instead of drying it. Cover the top only.

Stack it where the wind is, not in the tidiest corner of the yard. Sun helps; moving air helps more.

Trust a meter, not the calendar. Under 20% moisture on a freshly split face is dry. Above 25% you are boiling water instead of heating the house.

A useful tell with no tools: dry wood is noticeably lighter, its ends have checked and cracked, and two pieces knocked together ring rather than thud.
