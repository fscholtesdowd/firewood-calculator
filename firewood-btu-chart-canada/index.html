Species this site covers, by Canadian forest region

Natural Resources Canada groups the country into named forest regions. Some name an exact species. Others name only a genus, like "oak," which covers several species this site tracks separately. Each card below says which.

Where: northern Canada, coast to coast — the biggest forest region.

NRCan names: White Spruce, Black Spruce, Balsam Fir, Jack Pine, White Birch, Trembling Aspen, Tamarack, Willow.

Heat values are millions of BTU per full cord (128 cubic feet stacked) of air-dried wood at roughly 20% moisture content. Figures are taken from Utah State University Extension's Wood Heating firewood table, cross-checked where both cover a species against University of Illinois Extension's Characteristics of Firewood. Cord and rick dimensions, seasoned weight per cord, and the splitting, smoke and spark ratings draw on University of Missouri Extension G5450, Wood Fuel for Heating. Published tables disagree with each other by a million BTU or more on some species, and real output depends far more on how dry your wood actually is - treat these as a ranking and a planning estimate, not a laboratory measurement.

Shagbark Hickory carries about 1.9x the heat of Quaking Aspen per cord. Among species common in Canada, the spread is nearly as wide as the full table’s.

One rule that matters more than heat value: don’t move ash firewood

The Canadian Food Inspection Agency lists emerald-ash-borer zones in British Columbia, Manitoba, Ontario, Quebec, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia today. Inside those zones, the rule is blunt: “Moving ash firewood out of a regulated area is prohibited, whether for commercial or private purposes (such as taking firewood to the cottage or camping).”

Ash is one of the hottest-burning woods on the main chart. But if you’re buying or moving firewood in Canada, check the current regulated area first. That matters more than any BTU number on this page.

Source: Canadian Food Inspection Agency, "Regulated areas and items: Emerald ash borer" (checked 2026-09-18). Rule text quoted directly, not paraphrased — a fine or a seized load is the real cost of getting this one wrong.
