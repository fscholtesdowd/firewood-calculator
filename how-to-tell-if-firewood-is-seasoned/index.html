How to Tell If Firewood Is Seasoned (No Meter Needed)

Three checks with nothing but your hands and your ears - none of them alone is proof, but together they catch most green wood before it ruins a fire.

The three checks

CheckWhat to look/listen forCaveat
End cracksDeep radial cracks at the ends of split logs are one indicator of dryness.The article calls this alone difficult to judge by sight.
WeightSeasoned wood is noticeably lighter than unseasoned wood.Judging this by hand is subjective without something to compare it to.
SoundKnock two pieces together: dry wood rings, wet wood only thuds.—

Why none of these is the final word

University of New Hampshire Extension, "How can I tell if my firewood is seasoned and okay to burn?": "It can be difficult to tell if firewood has been seasoned by sight alone. The best and most reliable way to test firewood is with a wood moisture meter." That is the honest limit of a no-tool check - a piece can look checked and cracked at the ends and still be wet at the core, because the outside of a log dries first no matter how wet the inside still is. The sound test is the most reliable of the three because it is testing the whole piece, not just the end grain or your sense of its weight.

When it is worth the extra step

If the wood is going into a stove you are trying to get a clean secondary burn out of, or you are buying a load and cannot afford to guess, University of New Hampshire Extension, "How can I tell if my firewood is seasoned and okay to burn?" says the reliable path is a wood moisture meter, reading under 20% on a freshly split face - see the moisture meter guide for exactly how to take that reading.

Source: University of New Hampshire Extension, "How can I tell if my firewood is seasoned and okay to burn?" (2017-12-19).
